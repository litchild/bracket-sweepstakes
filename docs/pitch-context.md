# Pitch context

Background for anyone changing copy, content or features on the site. Summarized from the team's pitch deck,
the teammates' "Content & Regions" pitch, and the research done while building the prototype (fall 2026).

## The problem and the idea

- Chinese basketball fans follow the NBA and CBA. Most have never watched NCAA college basketball, do not know the
  schools, and have never filled out a bracket. "NCAA is too American" is the core problem the pitch names.
- In the US, much of March Madness viewing is driven by people with a personal stake: brackets and betting.
  Sports betting is illegal in China, so the pitch uses a free bracket challenge to create the same stake.
  A fan who has a bracket has a reason to watch games between schools they have never heard of.
- March Madness streams free on Bilibili, so the whole experience costs the fan nothing.
- If anyone ever wins a perfect-bracket prize, the story is marketing for the next year, because a verified perfect
  bracket has never happened.

## Who the site is for

- The fan persona: a Chinese basketball fan who knows the NBA well but knows nothing about March Madness, college
  seeding, brackets or the schools. Assume no prior knowledge. Explain terms like seed, upset, region, bracket,
  Sweet 16 and Final Four in plain language when they first appear.
- The people actually viewing the prototype are classmates and a professor in an English-language course, so the UI
  copy is English. Chinese characters are used for region names and the 中 badge.

## Prize

- China's Anti-Unfair Competition Law caps lottery-style prizes at 50,000 RMB, so the headline prize is 50,000 RMB.
- The deck frames the prize as options for ESPN China to choose from:
  - A: 50,000 RMB for a perfect bracket only. Expected cost is close to zero.
  - B: 50,000 RMB for a bracket that is perfect through the Sweet 16. It has happened only once on record.
  - C: 50,000 RMB for the best bracket. Certain cost, guaranteed winner.
- Do not mention the low odds in promotional copy. The promotion says "Win 50,000 RMB" and "Enter free."

## Dates

- Brackets open on Selection Sunday (in 2026 that was March 14) and lock before the first round (March 18 in 2026).
  The site uses the 2026 field and dates as placeholders until the 2027 bracket is announced.

## Precedents the deck cites

- Migu (China Mobile's sports platform) ran a prediction campaign that drew about 42 million participants, per
  People's Daily Overseas. They were participants, not viewers. This is the strongest proof that Chinese fans join
  free prediction games at scale.
- Kalshi's March Madness bracket in the US is the model for showing live crowd pick percentages: the first round
  shows the share of all brackets picking each team, and later rounds show picks among brackets still alive.

## Promotion

- Banner or pop-up ads inside Bilibili streams of the games before the tournament, especially games featuring
  Chinese players whose teams may not make the tournament. A fan watching their player sees the bracket ad and
  follows the tournament through the bracket.
- The ad mockup in the deck: headline "Win 50,000 RMB", the entry deadline, an "Enter free" button and a
  "Remind me" button. No mention of odds.

## Root for a region (teammates' pitch)

- Pitch title: "东南西北 Understand & Support a Region." Four regions, multiple ambassadors, one tournament.
- Why it works: a region stays alive as long as any of its teams does, the regions lean into what makes America
  interesting to Chinese fans, and real Chinese players act as ambassadors, so a region gains support even if those
  players are not on the court.
- Closing line of the pitch: "Chinese fans have always rooted for their province. This March, let's give them an
  American region to root for!"
- Video series per region: meet the ambassador, a day in their region, an end-of-video call to pick the region,
  then follow the region through the tournament.
- What each region shows fans:
  - East: lobster rolls, clam chowder, New York pizza, street basketball, New England campuses.
  - Midwest (the pitch calls it "North-ish"): Chicago deep-dish pizza, Wisconsin cheese curds, college-town life,
    county fairs, game-day traditions.
  - South: Oklahoma cowboy culture, Appalachian communities, barbecue, soul food, jazz.
  - West: beach life, Mexican-American food, Hollywood, big campuses.
- Ambassadors named in the pitch (Chinese players at American schools, men's and women's teams):
  East: Wang Junjie (UMass), Xinyi Li (LIU). Midwest: Kejia Ran (Xavier), Xiao "Smyle" Zhang (Iowa State),
  Sinan Huan (Purdue), Kai Yu (Indiana). South: Zhang Boyuan (Louisville), Deng Yuting (Oklahoma State),
  Zhu Zheng and Steven Huan (Western Carolina). West: Lin Wei (Oregon), Houran Dan (listed as Hawaii Pacific in the
  pitch; Sina Sports lists the University of Hawaii).

## Facts to keep straight

- 42 million is Migu's participant count, not a viewer count.
- The entry deadline is the day the first round starts, not Selection Sunday.
- The site is a student concept. It does not use ESPN or Bilibili logos and says it is not affiliated with ESPN,
  Bilibili or the NCAA.
